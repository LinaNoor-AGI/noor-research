## 4. Taxonomy of NSFG Singularities

The NSFG framework identifies five distinct types of singularities. Each arises from a different mechanism of maximal constraint on coherent traversal. They are not mathematical infinities; they are structural effects of the relational geometry—specifically, collapses of distinguishability within the coherence quotient.

**Definition 4.0 — Coherence Quotient Framework.** Before presenting the taxonomy, we establish the coherence quotient framework that grounds the definition of singularities. Let $\mathcal{M}$ be the Library. Define the equivalence relation $\sim_C$ by: $x \sim_C y$ iff $\forall O \in \mathrm{Obs}, \forall h \in H_O, \forall x_0 \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}}, \delta_O(x,y) = 0$. The coherence quotient $\mathcal{M}_C := \mathcal{M} / \sim_C$ defines the set of coherence-distinguishable equivalence classes. For a given observer $O$, the observer-relative quotient $\mathcal{M}_C^O := \mathcal{M} / \sim_O$ defines the classes distinguishable by that observer, where $x \sim_O y \iff \delta_O(x,y) = 0$. The observer-relative accessible quotient $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x) \subseteq \mathcal{M}_C^O$ is the subset of equivalence classes accessible from history $h$ and reference $x$. Each singularity type in the taxonomy below is a coherence-based collapse of distinguishability within $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)$, not a cardinality-based infinity in $\mathcal{M}$.

$$x \sim_C y \iff \forall O \in \mathrm{Obs}, \forall h \in H_O, \forall x_0 \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}}, \delta_O(x,y) = 0; \quad \mathcal{M}_C := \mathcal{M} / \sim_C; \quad x \sim_O y \iff \delta_O(x,y) = 0; \quad \mathcal{M}_C^O := \mathcal{M} / \sim_O; \quad \mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x) \subseteq \mathcal{M}_C^O$$

*The coherence quotient framework establishes that the Library may be infinite in cardinality, but coherence collapses indistinguishable copies. The observer only experiences the quotient, not the full Library. Singularities are collapses within this quotient.*

The taxonomy distinguishes between necessary singularities (which follow from the axiomatic floor) and conditional singularities (which depend on observer state, resolution, or traversal history). The XOR Fixed Point is necessary; the others are conditionally necessary or constructed. Each singularity type is a specific mode of coherence-based collapse within the observer-relative accessible quotient $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)$.

**The Five Singularity Types**

- XOR Fixed Point (𝒵): Termination — distinction collapses to the identity element 0. The echo returns to its source. This is a coherence-based collapse: all configurations that self-cancel to 0 are indistinguishable under $\sim_C$. The infinity of configurations that can reach 𝒵 collapses to a single equivalence class.
- Convergence Boundary: Destination Collapse — all coherent paths converge to a single equivalence class in $\mathcal{M}_C^O$. The river splits, but both branches reach the same sea. This is destination collapse, not path termination. The observer can traverse different paths, but all paths lead to the same coherence-distinguishable class.
- Recursive Oscillation: Failed Triadic Closure — the system loops through equivalence classes in $\mathcal{M}_C^O$ without resolving to a stable class. The loop never closes. This is a coherence-based failure: the dyad cannot stabilize because the coherence vectors do not sum to zero.
- Topological Singularity: Fracture — the accessible quotient $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)$ splits into disconnected components. The traveler falls through the floor. This is a coherence-based fracture: the observer's accessible reality splits, but the underlying Library $\mathcal{M}$ remains connected. Only the quotient $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)$ is disconnected.
- Pathway to Nowhere: Exact-Match Failure — the observer-relative accessible quotient $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)$ narrows to a single point as $I_N(O)$ approaches its maximum, but the required equivalence class is not in $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)$. The mirror shows no reflection. The required class may exist in $\mathcal{M}$ but is not coherence-accessible to observer $O$.

The list is complete for generic structural cases: every possible failure mode of the coherence relation within the observer-relative quotient $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)$ can be classified as termination, convergence, oscillation, topological fracture, or exact-match failure. Observer-specific variations may produce subcategories, but the structural taxonomy is exhaustive. This completeness follows from the structure of the coherence quotient: any collapse of distinguishability must manifest as one of these five modes.

The coherence quotient framework ensures that the taxonomy is grounded in the relationship between the Library $\mathcal{M}$ (which may be infinite) and the observer's accessible quotient $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)$ (which is finite for any finite-resolution observer). Singularities are not places where the Library breaks down; they are places where the observer's coherent relation to the Library becomes maximally constrained, collapsing distinguishability within the quotient.

**Formal Statement — Singularities as Coherence-Based Collapses.** Each singularity type is a collapse of distinguishability within $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)$. The Library $\mathcal{M}$ remains unchanged; the singularity is a feature of the observer-relative coherence quotient.

$$\mathcal{S} \ \mathrm{is\ a\ collapse\ of}\ \mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)\ \mathrm{,\ not\ a\ cardinality‐based\ infinity\ in}\ \mathcal{M}.$$

*The singularity is observer-relative and coherence-based. It does not imply that the Library breaks down or that infinite quantities exist in the quotient. The quotient collapses distinguishability, but the underlying Library remains complete.*

With the coherence quotient framework established and the five singularity types identified, the next step is to analyze the necessity and completeness of the taxonomy—to determine whether the list is complete and under what conditions, grounding the classification in the coherence quotient structure. The singularities are coherence-based collapses of distinguishability within $\mathcal{M}_C^O(h,x)$, not cardinality-based infinities in $\mathcal{M}$.
