### 9.3 Emergent Lawfulness Is Descriptive

At the axiomatic floor, no physical law has been supplied. The only primitive operation is XOR, and the Library is defined as its closure over the formal domain $D$.

Law-like regularity appears downstream when an observer encounters sufficiently stable recurrence among the transitions that remain accessible through coherent paths.

This construction does not assert that every regularity is universal, exact, or observer-independent. It establishes only that regularity can be described as an emergent property of constrained accessibility.

A later physical theory could impose additional structure and test whether particular emergent regularities correspond to known physical laws. Such a theory would constitute downstream NSFG work rather than part of this axiomatic floor.

**Derived regularity.**

$$L_O(x,y) = 1 \iff x \rightarrow y \text{ is a stable recurring transition across an appropriate family of O-accessible paths}$$

**Ensemble stability.**

$$P_O(y \mid x) \approx \mathrm{constant}$$

This subsection does not derive any particular physical law; it establishes only that law-like regularity can be described as an emergent property of constrained accessibility. Regularity is downstream of coherence and survivorship rather than a primitive of the axiomatic floor. A later physical theory may impose additional structure and test whether particular emergent regularities correspond to known physical laws, but such work belongs to downstream NSFG research rather than to the axiomatic floor itself.
