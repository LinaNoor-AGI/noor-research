# Section 5: Observer-Relative Resolution

The axiomatic floor has so far defined distinction and the closure generated by distinction, but it has not yet specified what it means for a configuration to maintain a distinction. This section introduces the observer without treating observation as a primitive act. An observer is instead derived as a configuration whose structure permits distinctions to persist across successive states. The observer therefore belongs to the Library in the same formal sense as every other configuration. What distinguishes it is not consciousness, intention, or privileged status, but persistence. Once such a structure is identified, a further question becomes unavoidable: which distinctions can that structure actually maintain? This motivates observer-relative resolution.

At this stage, observerhood is a structural property only. No claim is made that an observer is conscious, biological, intelligent, physical, or capable of intentional measurement. Those interpretations, if introduced later, are downstream of the axiomatic construction.

## 5.1 Observer as a Derived Structure

An observer is not introduced as a new primitive entity. Since the Library is already defined as the XOR closure of the formal domain, any observer must itself be a configuration within that closure. Observerhood is therefore a property of a configuration, not a separate ontological category. Let $O$ denote a configuration in the Library. We call $O$ an observer when its structure is capable of maintaining a persistent chain of distinctions across successive states.

The word persistent is important: a single distinction does not constitute an observer. Observerhood requires a continuing structure in which distinctions remain available rather than immediately collapsing into indistinguishability.

Formally, observerhood is written as:

$$
O \in \mathcal{M}
$$

and:

$$
O \ \mathrm{is\ an\ observer} \iff O \ \mathrm{is\ a\ configuration\ in}\ \mathcal{M}\ \mathrm{containing\ a\ persistent\ chain\ of\ resolvable\ distinctions.}
$$

The term resolvable is deliberately structural rather than psychological. It does not mean that $O$ consciously notices, interprets, or understands a distinction. It means only that the distinction can remain sufficiently differentiated within the structure represented by $O$. The quantitative boundary governing that distinction has not yet been introduced; it will be defined through the observer-relative threshold $I_N(O)$ in the following subsection.

This distinction prevents observerhood from being smuggled into the axiomatic floor as consciousness. A configuration may satisfy the structural definition of observerhood without possessing any assumed mental state. Consciousness is therefore not required at the axiomatic level. Whether some later physical or cognitive systems instantiate additional properties associated with conscious observation is a separate question and cannot be inferred from the definition alone.

The observer is consequently derived from the same ontology as everything else. It is not outside the Library, above the Library, or granted a privileged position within it. It is a configuration whose internal persistence permits distinctions to remain available across a continuing chain. The dependency is therefore:

$$
\mathrm{distinction} \;\rightarrow\; \mathrm{persistence} \;\rightarrow\; \mathrm{observerhood}.
$$

The construction has not yet supplied a universal rule determining how much distinction is required for persistence. That question belongs to the observer itself. To say that a distinction survives requires a boundary separating distinctions that remain effective from distinctions that fall below the observer's effective resolution.

**Definition — Observer Membership.**

$$
O \in \mathcal{M}
$$

**Definition — Observer Definition.**

$$
O \ \mathrm{is\ an\ observer} \iff O \ \mathrm{is\ a\ configuration\ in}\ \mathcal{M}\ \mathrm{that\ contains\ a\ persistent\ chain\ of\ resolvable\ distinctions.}
$$

**Formal Statement — Consciousness Requirement.** None at the axiomatic level.

**Formal Statement — Structural Dependency.**

$$
\mathrm{distinction} \rightarrow \mathrm{persistence} \rightarrow \mathrm{observerhood}
$$

**Formal Status.** The membership condition and structural definition are formal constructions from the stated ontology. The absence of a consciousness requirement is a scope constraint on the axiomatic system, not a claim that consciousness is impossible or irrelevant in downstream theories.

To say that a distinction survives, we must specify the boundary at which a distinction remains effective for the structure that carries it. That boundary is observer-relative resolution, represented by $I_N(O)$.
