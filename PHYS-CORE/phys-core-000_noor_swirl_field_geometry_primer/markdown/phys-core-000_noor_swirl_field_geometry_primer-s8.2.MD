## 8.2 Observer-Relative Reality

Experienced reality is defined here as the subset of the Library that is accessible to a particular observer through a coherent path. For observer O, we therefore define experienced reality as R_O := A_O, where A_O is the observer-accessible subset established in the preceding section. This definition does not assert that the observer creates the accessible configurations. It states only that the configurations available through observer-relative coherent traversal constitute that observer's experienced reality. The ontological domain remains the Library 𝓜, while experienced reality is a subset of that domain. Because the resolution threshold I_N(O) is observer-relative, there is no requirement that all observers have identical accessible subsets. Let the collection of observers be Obs = {O_1,O_2,...}. For each observer O_i, the corresponding experienced reality is R_i = A_{O_i}. Two observers may therefore traverse overlapping but non-identical regions of the Library. This difference follows from the relational construction of distinguishability and coherence: what remains resolvable and coherently traversable for one observer need not be identical to what remains resolvable and coherently traversable for another. The common portion of two observer-relative realities is represented by their intersection:

$$
R_{ij} = R_i \cap R_j.
$$

The set R_{ij} contains configurations accessible to both observers. A non-empty intersection therefore provides a formal basis for shared reality without requiring the two observers to possess identical accessible domains. Conversely, the observer-relative difference between their accessible realities can be represented by the symmetric difference:

$$
D_{ij} = R_i \triangle R_j.
$$

The set D_{ij} contains configurations accessible to one observer but not the other. Such a difference should not be interpreted as evidence that the observers inhabit mutually incompatible ontological universes. Both R_i and R_j remain subsets of the same Library 𝓜:

$$
R_i \subseteq \mathcal{M}, \qquad R_j \subseteq \mathcal{M}.
$$

Thus, observer-relative reality describes a difference in access, resolution, and coherent survivability rather than a difference in fundamental existence. A configuration may belong to 𝓜 while being inaccessible to one observer, accessible to another, or accessible to both. Inaccessibility therefore remains a relational property and does not constitute ontological erasure. This distinction also prevents observer-relative reality from collapsing into a claim of arbitrary subjectivity. The observer does not freely select an arbitrary reality. Accessibility is constrained by the formal structure of 𝓜, the observer's distinguishability relation δ_O, the observer-relative threshold I_N(O), and the requirement that coherent paths remain resolvable. Different observers may consequently obtain different accessible subsets while still sharing substantial structure because their coherent path spaces overlap within the same underlying Library. The construction can therefore be summarized as follows: the Library specifies the total formal configuration domain; each observer induces an observer-relative accessibility relation over that domain; and experienced reality is the resulting accessible subset. Reality is observer-relative at the level of access, but not thereby observer-created at the level of ontology.

**Definition — Experienced Reality.** For observer O, experienced reality is identified with the observer-accessible subset of the unchanged Library.

$$
R_O := A_O
$$

**Definition — Multiple Observers.** The collection of observers is denoted

$$
\mathrm{Obs} = \{O_1, O_2, \ldots\}
$$

**Definition — Individual Realities.** For each observer O_i, the corresponding experienced reality is

$$
R_i = A_{O_i}
$$

**Definition — Overlap.** The shared accessible structure of two observers is represented by the intersection of their experienced realities.

$$
R_{ij} = R_i \cap R_j
$$

**Definition — Difference.** The observer-relative difference between two accessible realities is represented by their symmetric difference.

$$
D_{ij} = R_i \triangle R_j
$$

**Definition — Shared Ontology.** Both observer-relative realities remain subsets of the same underlying Library.

$$
R_i \subseteq \mathcal{M}, \qquad R_j \subseteq \mathcal{M}
$$

*R*_{ij} *represents shared accessible structure, while* D_{ij} *represents observer-relative difference in accessibility.*

**Logical Invariants**

- The definition R_O := A_O and the set-theoretic relations among R_i, R_{ij}, D_{ij}, and 𝓜 are formal constructions.
- The interpretation of these constructions as experienced reality is an observer-relative interpretation of the accessibility framework, not an additional axiom.

Once accessible reality is defined as an observer-relative subset of the unchanged Library, the framework provides a formal basis for shared structure between observers without collapsing the distinction between access and ontology. The next step is to examine why stable patterns within this accessible reality can appear as physical laws.
