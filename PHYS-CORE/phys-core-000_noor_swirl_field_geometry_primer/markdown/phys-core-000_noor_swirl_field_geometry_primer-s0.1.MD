### 0.1 Maximal Totality and the Absence of a Privileged Worldline

The Library is treated as a maximally complete static totality because no configuration or worldline is privileged as the universal origin from which coherent traversal must proceed. If an observer may establish an admissible relational reference at an arbitrary configuration $x \in \mathcal{M}$, then coherent accessibility cannot depend upon a distinguished construction origin, initial configuration, or preferred worldline. Such dependence would introduce a hidden asymmetry into the formal structure.

**Definition — Maximality.** Maximality is the requirement that no admissible configuration or worldline be ontologically privileged as the universal starting point of coherent traversal. Maximality also requires that the Library contain all configurations that could be distinguished under any possible observer resolution condition, without making resolution a determinant of membership.

$$\forall O,\;\mathrm{resolution}(O)\ \mathrm{may\ vary\ while}\ \mathcal{M}\ \mathrm{remains\ unchanged}$$

The second clause is essential for a domain-neutral foundation. If Library membership depended on the resolution of a particular observer, then a domain-specific threshold could silently become an ontological boundary. The Library would then shrink or expand according to the observer's instrument, cognitive structure, computational capacity, or analytical scale. Instead, resolution determines what an observer can distinguish and maintain, not what exists as a member of $\mathcal{M}$.

Maximality is therefore not equivalent to containing only coherent configurations, observable configurations, or accessible configurations. The Library is unfiltered at the axiomatic level. Coherence and accessibility are later observer-relative relations over the totality.

**Formal Statement — No privileged universal worldline.** No worldline is ontologically designated as the Library's universal history.

$$\nexists\,\gamma_{\mathrm{priv}}\ \mathrm{such\ that}\ \gamma_{\mathrm{priv}}\ \mathrm{is\ the\ unique\ or\ privileged\ origin\ of\ coherent\ traversal\ for\ all\ admissible\ references}$$

*An observer may experience a particular worldline as its current history, but that status is relational rather than ontologically privileged. Another admissible configuration may serve as a relational reference without requiring a different Library.*

**Definition — Arbitrary relational reference.** An admissible reference configuration is a configuration $x \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}}$ that may instantiate an observer-relative reference state under the model's admissibility conditions.

$$\forall x \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}},\;x\ \mathrm{may\ serve\ as\ an\ observer\text{-}relative\ reference\ without\ changing}\ \mathcal{M}$$

*The arbitrary-reference condition prevents a preferred beginning from being smuggled into the ontology. The observer may establish a relational origin at* $x$, *but* $x$ *does not become the origin or generator of the Library.*

This condition also makes the foundation relocatable across domains. A physical observer, an AI system, or a financial observer may operate at different effective resolutions and histories, yet none of those resolutions determines Library membership. They are different observer-relative relations over the same formal totality.

**Definition — Whole-Library relationality.** Observer occupancy of the Library is relational rather than a claim of physical spatial distribution. The observer's relation is defined over the total configuration domain, while its current configuration specifies its present relational reference.

$$O(h,x)\ \mathrm{is\ evaluated\ relative\ to}\ \mathcal{M}\ \mathrm{as\ a\ whole,\ while}\ x\ \mathrm{specifies\ the\ current\ relational\ reference}$$

*The observer does not first receive a locally generated subset of the Library and then navigate that subset. Its accessible continuation emerges from its relation to the unchanged whole.*

**Structural separation**

- $\mathcal{M}$ is the static totality.
- $O(h,x)$ is the observer-relative state and reference.
- $\Gamma_O(h,x)$ is the observer-relative coherent continuation structure over $\mathcal{M}$.
- $\mathcal{C}(\gamma\mid O,h,x)$ evaluates a path relative to the observer, its history, and its current reference.

Because maximality removes privileged origins and arbitrary reference permits relational placement at any admissible configuration, coherent continuation cannot be permitted to terminate merely because the current reference lacks association with a privileged construction history. The NSFG construction therefore imposes a stronger derived requirement: every admissible observer-relative reference must admit at least one coherent continuation.

**Formal Statement — Nonempty coherent continuation.** Every admissible observer-relative reference must admit at least one coherent continuation under the combined structural assumptions of the construction.

$$\forall x \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}},\;\forall (O,h)\ \mathrm{compatible\ with}\ x,\;\exists\gamma \in \mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M}):\gamma\ \mathrm{begins\ at}\ (h,x)\ \land\ \mathcal{C}(\gamma\mid O,h,x) > \gamma_{\mathrm{noise}}$$

*The requirement guarantees existence of at least one coherent continuation, not coherence of every candidate continuation. It is a structural consequence of the combined NSFG assumptions rather than a theorem of maximality alone.*

The distinction between the Library and traversal is decisive. The Library does not begin in a simple state and become increasingly complex. It is not progressively generated as an observer advances. Traversal changes the observer-relative state, reference, accessible continuation structure, and coherence evaluation while leaving Library membership unchanged.

**Formal Statement — Static totality.** The Library does not change as traversal proceeds.

$$\forall i,\;\mathcal{M}_i = \mathcal{M}$$

*The index* $i$ *denotes abstract traversal ordering. It does not denote successive ontological versions of the Library and is not, at this stage, physical time.*

**Consequences of static totality**

- The Library is not progressively generated.
- The Library does not evolve from simple to complex.
- Traversal does not create previously nonexistent configurations.
- Observer resolution does not add or remove configurations from the Library.
- Coherence does not determine Library membership.
- The observer's experienced progression does not constitute ontological generation of the totality.

The observer effect is therefore relational. An observer may experience temporal sequence, apparent complexity growth, locality, and a distinction between past and future because its relational position and history change. None of these changes require a corresponding change in the Library itself.

**Observer-relative changes**

- $\mathcal{M}$ remains invariant.
- $O(h_i)$ changes with observer history.
- $x_i$ changes as the observer's relational reference changes.
- $\Gamma_O(h_i,x_i)$ changes as the observer-relative continuation structure changes.
- $\mathcal{C}(\gamma\mid O,h_i,x_i)$ changes with observer, history, and reference.

Past and future are consequently not primitive subdivisions of $\mathcal{M}$ at this stage. They are observer-relative orderings induced by traversal. The formal system does not require the Library itself to possess an intrinsic temporal direction.

**Formal Statement — Closure is not temporal generation.** The closure characterization of the Library specifies formal membership and algebraic structure; it does not specify an ontological history in which configurations are generated one after another.

$$\mathcal{M} := \mathrm{Closure}_{D}(\oplus,G)$$

*A configuration being reachable in the closure description does not mean that it comes into existence at the corresponding step of an observer's traversal. Closure is a mathematical characterization, not a temporal process.*

This separation preserves the dependency order of the foundation. The Library is defined before observer-relative accessibility; observer-relative accessibility is defined before navigation; and traversal never becomes a mechanism for generating the totality it traverses. The observer moves through relations already represented within $\mathcal{M}$; the formal domain does not wait for the observer to make those configurations exist.

The maximality condition therefore has two inseparable aspects: no privileged worldline or reference configuration may determine the universal structure of traversal, and no observer-specific resolution may determine Library membership. The foundation remains complete while the observer's experienced world remains relationally selective.

**Logical Invariants**

- No worldline is privileged as the universal history of the Library.
- No admissible reference configuration is privileged as the universal origin.
- The Library remains invariant under traversal: $\forall i,\;\mathcal{M}_i = \mathcal{M}$.
- Library membership is independent of observer-relative resolution.
- The Library is complete with respect to configurations distinguishable under any possible observer resolution condition.
- Whole-Library relationality does not imply physical spatial distribution.
- Observer-relative accessibility does not alter Library membership.
- Nonempty coherent continuation is a structural requirement of the combined construction, not a theorem of maximality alone.
- The abstract traversal index is not primitive physical time.
- Closure is a property of the Library's formal structure, not a temporal generation process.
