## 9.2 Repeated Survivorship

Consider a family of paths evaluated relative to an observer $O$, its relevant history $h$, and current reference configuration $x$. These paths are drawn from the unchanged total path domain $\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})$. A transition that repeatedly survives observer-relative coherence across an appropriate family of such paths can exhibit stable recurrence relative to $O$.

Survivorship therefore operates on traversal relations, not on the ontology of the Library. A transition may fail to remain coherent for observer $O$ without the configurations or candidate paths involved thereby being removed from $\mathcal{M}$ or from the total path domain. The incoherent complement remains represented in the Library; only its continuation through the observer-maintained coherent relation is not retained as part of that traversal.

**What repeated survivorship does and does not establish**

- It identifies recurrence within an observer-accessible ensemble.
- It can support a derived description of stable regularity.
- It does not add a governing rule to the axiomatic floor.
- It does not alter membership in $\mathcal{M}$.
- It does not erase paths that fail the observer's coherence condition.
- It does not establish that the observed recurrence is universal or observer-independent.

The important distinction is between requiring a transition and observing that a transition repeatedly survives. The former would introduce a governing rule into the axiomatic floor; the latter is a descriptive identification of a stable pattern within the relational structure produced by observer-relative coherence.

Thus, a transition does not become regular because an external law commands it to occur. It becomes law-like when its recurrence is stable across an appropriate observer-accessible ensemble. Such regularity is therefore derived from repeated survivorship rather than imposed upon the Library.

Survivorship is the mechanism by which domain-specific regularities emerge from the domain-neutral foundation. In physical contexts, survivorship corresponds to the persistence of physical laws across observations; in AI contexts, to the stability of symbolic patterns across reasoning steps; in financial contexts, to the recurrence of market trends across trading periods. The structural mechanism is the same in all domains.

The existence of at least one coherent continuation from an admissible reference configuration is a separate structural requirement of the model. It does not imply that every candidate continuation survives, nor does it follow from recurrence itself. Survivorship may restrict the observer's experienced continuation relation while leaving the complete Library unchanged.

The relevant regularity remains relational: it is regularity as established within the observer's accessible and resolution-maintained traversal structure, not necessarily an observer-independent law imposed upon the entire Library. The totality remains maximally complete regardless of which transitions survive for a particular observer.

**Definition — Stable recurring transition.** A transition $x \rightarrow y$ is treated as stably recurring for observer $O$ when it repeatedly survives observer-relative coherence across an appropriate family of $O$-accessible paths evaluated over the unchanged Library $\mathcal{M}$.

$$L_O(x,y)=1 \iff x\rightarrow y \ \mathrm{is\ a\ stable\ recurring\ transition\ across\ an\ appropriate\ family\ of\ } O \mathrm{-accessible\ paths\ evaluated\ over\ } \mathcal{M}.$$

*$L_O$ records a descriptive regularity in the observer's accessible traversal structure. It does not impose the transition upon the Library.*

**Conditional recurrence.**

$$P_O(y\mid x)\approx\mathrm{constant}$$

*The expression captures recurrence statistically or descriptively when an appropriate ensemble has been defined. It is not itself an axiomatic probability law.*

**Definition — Surviving continuation set.** The observer-relative surviving continuation set consists of those paths in the unfiltered continuation space that remain coherent for the relevant observer and traversal context.

$$\Omega_O^{\mathrm{survive}}(x)=\{\gamma\in\Omega(x)\mid\mathcal{C}(\gamma\mid O,h,x)=1\}$$

*Survivorship selects the continuations maintained by the observer-relative traversal relation. It does not remove the non-surviving continuations from* $\Omega(x)$ *or from* $\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})$.

**Formal Statement — Survivorship does not alter the totality.** For any observer $O$, the surviving continuation set is a subset of the complete continuation space, which itself consists of paths contained in the unchanged Library.

$$\Omega_O^{\mathrm{survive}}(x)\subseteq\Omega(x)\subseteq\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})$$

*Observer-relative survivorship changes accessibility and traversal, not Library membership.*

**Formal Statement — Nonempty coherent continuation.** For an admissible reference configuration and an observer state that permits continuation, the coherent continuation set remains nonempty.

$$\forall x\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}},\;\Gamma_O(h,x)\neq\varnothing$$

*This requirement guarantees continuation without requiring every candidate path to survive and without treating recurrence as the source of continuation.*

**Logical Invariants**

- The Library $\mathcal{M}$ remains unchanged under observer-relative survivorship.
- Survivorship restricts accessibility rather than Library membership.
- The unfiltered continuation space remains represented even when particular paths fail observer-relative coherence.
- Repeated survivorship can produce descriptive regularity without becoming an axiom.
- $L_O$ is observer-relative and is not automatically universal.
- Domain-specific regularities can arise from the same domain-neutral survivorship mechanism.
- The existence of a coherent continuation is distinct from recurrence of a particular transition.
- No surviving path becomes ontologically privileged merely by recurring.

Stable recurrence is now identified as a descriptive feature of observer-accessible traversal, not a governing principle imposed on the Library. What remains is to distinguish this emergent lawfulness from any axiom, and to show that the regularity of surviving paths is a consequence of the survivorship relation rather than a premise standing behind it.
