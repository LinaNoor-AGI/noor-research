## 6.2 Persistence Across Successive Distinctions and Observer-Relative Coherence

Let $\gamma=(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ be a finite sequence of configurations in $\mathcal{M}$. The index $i$ supplies an abstract ordering of successive configurations. It is not identified with physical time, and the sequence does not represent the progressive creation of Library states.

Every $x_i$ belongs to the same unchanged Library $\mathcal{M}$. Traversal through the sequence therefore describes a relation among configurations already contained in the static totality. The Library does not acquire new members as the sequence advances, nor does a prior configuration become absent because a later configuration is traversed.

**Definition — Resolvable transition.** A transition from $x_i$ to $x_{i+1}$ is resolvable by observer $O$ when the observer-relative distinction between the two configurations reaches or exceeds the observer's resolution floor.

$$\delta_O(x_i,x_{i+1})\geq I_N(O)$$

**Definition — Finite persistence.** A finite sequence is persistent for $O$ when every successive distinction in the sequence remains resolvable.

$$P_O(\gamma,n)=1 \iff \forall i<n,\;\delta_O(x_i,x_{i+1})\geq I_N(O)$$

*Persistence is the maintenance of observer-resolvable distinction across an ordered chain rather than the occurrence of a single distinguishable transition.*

Persistence is not a guarantee of continuity in the physical sense; it is a structural property of observer-relative traversal. In physical domains, persistence may correspond to worldline continuity. In AI domains, it may correspond to symbolic coherence across reasoning steps. In financial domains, it may correspond to trend persistence across market states. The structural definition is the same; the interpretation is domain-specific.

This domain-neutrality is a constraint on interpretation, not a claim that physics, AI, and finance contain the same objects or obey the same empirical laws. The commonality lies at the formal level: in each case, persistence concerns the maintenance of resolvable successive distinctions relative to an observer structure and its resolution condition.

**Definition — Structural observerhood.** Observerhood is identified wherever a configuration or relational structure can maintain a persistent chain of resolvable distinctions relative to its observer-specific resolution condition.

$$O\in\mathrm{Obs}\subseteq\mathcal{M} \iff O\ \mathrm{can\ maintain\ a\ persistent\ chain\ of\ resolvable\ distinctions}$$

*The observer is a structural designation within the Library, not a privileged subject introduced from outside it.*

Nothing in this definition requires consciousness, perception, intention, agency, or a human subject. Observerhood names a structural capacity to preserve distinguishability across an abstractly ordered sequence. A human observer may instantiate such a structure, but the formal definition does not privilege humans or conscious systems.

The identification of an observer does not represent a physical birth event or a temporal transition in the Library. It is a structural designation made within the static totality. Wherever the relevant persistent distinguishability structure exists, observer-capable relational structure can be identified without introducing an additional ontological event.

The observer established by persistent distinguishability is not thereby confined to a locally generated region of $\mathcal{M}$. Subsequent observer-relative coherence is evaluated over paths in the whole Library. The observer's history and current relational reference can change the resulting accessibility relation while leaving the underlying totality unchanged.

**Definition — Observer-relative coherence relation.** Coherence extends persistence from individual successive transitions to paths evaluated relative to the observer, the relevant observer history, and the current relational reference configuration.

$$\mathcal{C}(\gamma\mid O,h,x)$$

*The notation makes explicit that coherence is relational. The same represented path may be coherent under one observer-relative condition and not coherent under another.*

Where $h$ denotes the relevant observer history and $x$ denotes the current observer-relative reference configuration, these parameters modify the relational evaluation of a path; they do not modify Library membership. Relocating the reference from $x$ to another admissible configuration $x'$ therefore changes the observer's relation to the Library without creating a new Library or invoking an external reference frame.

**Definition — Whole-Library path domain.** Candidate paths for coherence are drawn from the path structure of the unchanged Library.

$$\gamma\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})$$

*Observer-relative filtering acts on paths already represented within* $\mathcal{M}$*; it does not construct a local ontological neighborhood from which the observer subsequently reasons.*

**Definition — Observer-relative coherent continuation.** The coherent continuation set contains paths in the whole Library that extend the relevant history from the current reference and satisfy the observer-relative coherence condition above the model's operative coherence floor.

$$\Gamma_O(h,x)=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})\mid \gamma\ \mathrm{extends}\ h\ \mathrm{from}\ x\ \mathrm{and}\ \mathcal{C}(\gamma\mid O,h,x)>\gamma_{\mathrm{noise}}\}$$

*The continuation set is a relationally selected subset of whole-Library paths. Non-selected paths remain represented in the Library and are not ontologically deleted.*

Under the combined NSFG structural assumptions, every admissible observer-relative reference configuration is required to admit at least one coherent continuation above the relevant observer-dependent coherence floor. This requirement is deliberately stated as a derived structural condition of the combined model rather than as a theorem obtained from maximality alone.

**Formal Statement — Nonempty continuation requirement.** For every admissible observer-relative reference configuration, the coherent continuation space is nonempty.

$$\forall x\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}},\;\Gamma_O(h,x)\neq\varnothing$$

*The condition ensures that admissible observer-relative traversal does not terminate merely because of the coordinate of the current reference within the unbounded Library. It does not assert that every possible path is coherent.*

Persistence therefore supplies the bridge from isolated distinguishability to observerhood. Once persistent distinguishability identifies an observer-capable structure, coherence can be defined as the observer-relative evaluation of paths through the unchanged whole Library. The observer, relevant history, and current relational reference determine the relation; none of them changes the underlying totality.

**Domain-neutral structural interpretation**

- Physics: persistence may correspond to continuity of a physical relation such as a worldline.
- AI: persistence may correspond to symbolic continuity across successive reasoning states.
- Finance: persistence may correspond to the continuation of a trend or relational pattern across market states.
- Other domains: the same formal persistence relation may be instantiated whenever successive distinctions can remain resolvable relative to an observer structure.

**Logical invariants preserved**

- The Library $\mathcal{M}$ remains static under traversal.
- The sequence index $i$ is abstract ordering, not physical time.
- Persistence requires repeated resolvability across successive distinctions.
- Observerhood is structural rather than primitive.
- Observerhood does not imply consciousness or agency.
- The resolution floor remains observer-relative through $I_N(O)$.
- Coherence is relational rather than intrinsic to an isolated path.
- Candidate coherent paths belong to $\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})$.
- Observer-relative filtering does not alter Library membership.
- The existence of coherent continuation from every admissible reference configuration is a combined structural requirement, not a consequence of maximality alone.

**Logical Invariants**

- Persistence is defined over an abstract sequence, not physical time.
- Every configuration in the sequence remains a member of the same static Library $\mathcal{M}$.
- Persistence requires repeated observer-relative resolvability.
- Observerhood is structural and does not constitute an additional primitive axiom.
- Observerhood does not imply consciousness, perception, agency, or intentionality.
- Persistence has a domain-neutral formal structure while its interpretation is domain-specific.
- Coherence is relational: $\mathcal{C}(\gamma\mid O,h,x)$.
- Candidate paths for coherence are drawn from the whole Library.
- Changing observer history or reference changes relational evaluation without changing Library membership.
- The nonempty coherent-continuation requirement belongs to the combined structural model and is not claimed as a theorem of maximality alone.

The section has established persistence as a domain-neutral structural property — a chain of successive distinctions that remain resolvable at or above an observer's resolution floor — and has identified observerhood structurally as the capacity to maintain such a chain. With persistence and observerhood now in place, coherence can be defined as the observer-, history-, and reference-relative evaluation of paths through the unchanged whole Library: coherence is not an intrinsic property of a path, but a relation between that path and the observer that evaluates it. The Library itself remains static throughout, and the next construction extends this relational structure from single transitions to whole paths.
