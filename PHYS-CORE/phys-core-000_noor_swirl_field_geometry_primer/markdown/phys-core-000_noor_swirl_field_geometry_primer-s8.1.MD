### 8.1 Accessibility Is Not Existence

The distinction between accessibility and existence must remain exact. Membership in 𝓜 is determined by the formal characterization of the Library. Accessibility is determined by whether a configuration participates in at least one observer-relative coherent traversal. The first relation concerns the static totality; the second concerns the observer's relation to that totality.

**Definition — Admissible Reference Configuration.** Let $x$ be a configuration in 𝓜 capable of instantiating an observer-relative reference state under the model's admissibility conditions.

$$x \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}}$$

*The reference configuration supplies the relational origin for evaluating accessibility. It is not an ontological origin or generator of* 𝓜.

The observer is relationally situated with respect to the whole Library 𝓜 rather than ontologically confined to a local region of it. The reference $x$ therefore identifies where the observer-relative evaluation begins, not a place where the Library itself begins.

**Reference-Frame Neutrality.**

$$x_1,x_2 \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}} \Rightarrow \mathcal{M}[x_1] = \mathcal{M}[x_2] = \mathcal{M}$$

*Changing the reference may alter the observer-relative accessibility relation, but it does not alter the underlying totality.*

No configuration is privileged as the universal starting point of coherent traversal. If $x$ and $x'$ are both admissible observer-relative reference configurations, changing the reference changes the relational evaluation without changing Library membership. A privileged construction origin would introduce an asymmetry into traversal that is not present in the foundational ontology.

Reference-point neutrality does not, by itself, prove that every possible reference admits a coherent continuation. The stronger requirement must therefore be stated explicitly. The model requires that every admissible observer-relative reference configuration remain capable of at least one coherent continuation for every compatible observer and history.

**Formal Statement — Reference-Point Coherence Proposition.** Every admissible observer-relative reference configuration must admit at least one coherent continuation under the observer and history compatible with that reference.

$$\forall x \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}},\ \forall(O,h)\ \mathrm{compatible\ with}\ x,\ \exists \gamma \in \mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M}): \gamma\ \mathrm{begins\ at}\ (h,x) \land \mathcal{C}(\gamma \mid O,h,x)=1$$

*This is a derived model requirement arising from the combined requirements of maximal static totality, reference-point neutrality, whole-Library relationality, and continued coherent traversal from arbitrary admissible references. It is not claimed to follow from maximality alone.*

The proposition therefore does not say that every path from every configuration is coherent. It says only that an admissible reference cannot be a dead end solely because of its location in the Library. Coherent traversal must remain possible without introducing a privileged origin or an observer-independent boundary.

**Observer-Relative Accessibility.**

$$A_O(h,x) = \{y \in \mathcal{M} \mid \exists \gamma \in \mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})\ \mathrm{beginning\ at}\ (h,x)\ \mathrm{and\ containing}\ y\ \mathrm{such\ that}\ \mathcal{C}(\gamma \mid O,h,x)=1\}$$

*The accessible subset emerges from the relation between the observer, its history, its reference, and the static Library.*

The resulting construction is explicitly non-idealistic. The observer does not create $A_O(h,x)$ as new ontology. Rather, $A_O(h,x)$ is a subset relation over configurations already contained in 𝓜. Likewise, a configuration outside $A_O(h,x)$ remains a member of 𝓜 unless independently excluded by the formal characterization of the Library.

The same distinction permits domain-neutral application. A physical observer may access a subset of physical configurations; an AI system may access a subset of a knowledge or reasoning domain; a financial observer may access a subset of actionable market states. In each case the formal relation is unchanged: accessibility is determined relative to an observer, its current relational conditions, and the coherent continuations available within the underlying totality.

The observer's reference therefore functions as a relational anchor rather than a privileged origin. Moving the reference from $x$ to $x'$ can alter which configurations are accessible through coherent traversal, but it cannot alter 𝓜 itself. This is the required separation between observer-relative worldline structure and Library ontology.

**Example.** *Setup:* Suppose $x$ and $y$ are both configurations in the unchanged Library 𝓜, with $x \in$ 𝓜_admissible. Assume there exists a path $\gamma_x$ beginning from $(h,x)$ such that 𝓒($\gamma_x$ | $O,h,x$)$=1$, while no currently accessible $O$-coherent path from $(h,x)$ contains $y$. *Result:* Then $x \in$ 𝓜, $y \in$ 𝓜, $y \notin A_O(h,x)$, and a coherent continuation from $x$ remains available. *Interpretation:* The example demonstrates that accessibility is not existence. The observer's traversal from $x$ identifies a restricted relational subset of the unchanged totality; it does not generate that subset as new ontology. Likewise, inability to access $y$ does not remove $y$ from 𝓜.

**Example.** *Setup:* Let $x_1$ and $x_2$ be two admissible reference configurations in 𝓜. *Result:* The observer-relative accessibility relations $A_O(h,x_1)$ and $A_O(h,x_2)$ may differ, while the underlying Library remains 𝓜 in both cases. *Interpretation:* Neither reference is a privileged universal origin. The difference belongs to the observer-relative traversal relation rather than to the ontology of the Library.

No intrinsic past/future partition is introduced into 𝓜 by this construction. The sequence of configurations encountered by an observer constitutes an ordering relative to its history and reference. Apparent temporal and complexity ordering therefore belongs to observer-relative traversal rather than to evolution of the Library itself.

The formal boundary is consequently narrow. This subsection establishes accessibility, reference-point neutrality, and the requirement of coherent continuation from admissible references. It does not yet introduce emergent physical law, probabilistic dynamics, consciousness, physical time, or cosmological interpretation.

**Logical Invariants**

- 𝓜 is a single static totality.
- ∀$i$, 𝓜$_i$ = 𝓜.
- Closure is a mathematical property and not temporal generation.
- The observer is relationally situated with respect to the whole Library.
- $A_O(h,x) \subseteq$ 𝓜.
- $y \notin A_O(h,x)$ does not imply $y \notin$ 𝓜.
- Accessibility does not create ontological membership.
- Inaccessibility does not erase ontological membership.
- Changing the admissible reference changes the observer-relative relation but does not alter 𝓜.
- No privileged worldline or universal construction origin is assumed.
- Every admissible reference configuration is required to admit at least one coherent continuation.
- The Reference-Point Coherence Proposition is a derived model requirement, not a theorem of maximality alone.
- Accessibility is structurally domain-neutral.
- Physical, AI, and financial interpretations differ in content, not in the underlying accessibility relation.
- No consciousness requirement is introduced.

Having separated accessibility from existence and grounded reference-point neutrality in the unchanged Library, the next section turns to the filtering of continuation paths by observer-relative survivorship, and to the emergent regularities that recurring survivorship can produce without deleting the alternatives the Library already contains.
