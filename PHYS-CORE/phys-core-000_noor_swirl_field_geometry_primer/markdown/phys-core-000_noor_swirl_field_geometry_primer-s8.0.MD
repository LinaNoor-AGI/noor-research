## 8. Accessible Reality

The Library, 𝓜, is defined at the axiomatic floor as the XOR closure of the generating configurations within the formal domain D. Its membership is therefore determined by the formal characterization of the totality: a configuration either belongs to 𝓜 or it does not. The closure relation is mathematical rather than temporal. It does not describe a process by which the Library is progressively generated, expanded, or traversed into existence.

Observer-relative accessibility introduces a different question. Rather than asking whether a configuration belongs to the Library, we ask whether that configuration occurs within a path whose distinctions remain resolvable by a particular observer O. Accessibility therefore concerns the relation between an observer and the unchanged totality. It is not a second membership criterion.

**Definition — Observer-Relative Accessibility.** For observer O, relevant history h, and admissible reference configuration x, $A_O(h,x)$ is the subset of 𝓜 containing configurations that occur on at least one path beginning from $(h,x)$ whose transitions remain coherent relative to O.

$$A_O(h,x) = \{y \in \mathcal{M} \mid \exists \gamma \in \mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})\ \mathrm{beginning\ at}\ (h,x)\ \mathrm{and\ containing}\ y\ \mathrm{such\ that}\ \mathcal{C}(\gamma \mid O,h,x)=1\}$$

*The accessible subset is determined by coherent traversal relative to the observer, its relevant history, and its current relational reference. It is not generated by the observer.*

The distinction between membership and accessibility is fundamental. The condition $y \in \mathcal{M}$ concerns the formal status of y within the static totality. The condition $y \in A_O(h,x)$ concerns whether y can be reached through a coherent observer-relative traversal from the specified history and reference. These conditions are therefore not equivalent.

**Non-Erasure.**

$$y \notin A_O(h,x) \nRightarrow y \notin \mathcal{M}$$

*Failure of accessibility does not imply absence from the Library.*

A configuration may remain fully contained within 𝓜 while being inaccessible to a particular observer under a particular relational condition.

The Library is consequently not reduced when an observer has access to only a restricted subset. The observer's resolution, history, and relational reference determine which distinctions can participate in a coherent traversal, while 𝓜 remains unchanged. The observer does not bring inaccessible configurations into existence by resolving them, nor does failure to resolve a configuration remove it from 𝓜.

This also establishes a domain-neutral interpretation of accessibility. $A_O(h,x)$ is the subset of the Library accessible to observer O under its current relational conditions. In a physical domain, this may correspond to an observer-accessible set of physical configurations or observable states. In an AI domain, it may correspond to the knowledge or reasoning context available to a system. In a financial domain, it may correspond to the set of actionable market states available under the observer's current informational and relational conditions. The structural definition is the same; only the interpretation of what counts as accessible content changes.

This domain neutrality does not require the accessible subsets of different observers or domains to be identical. It states only that the formal relation is invariant: accessibility is evaluated as an observer-relative relation over the same Library structure. Domain-specific meaning enters through the interpretation of configurations, observer state, resolution, history, and actionable continuation, not through a new foundational ontology.

**Definition — Experienced Reality.** For the purposes of the primer, observer-relative experienced reality is identified with the accessible subset of the Library.

$$R_O(h,x) := A_O(h,x)$$

$R_O(h,x)$ *names the portion of the unchanged Library available through coherent traversal relative to observer O, history h, and reference x. This notation does not identify the accessible subset with the whole Library.*

The observer is therefore relationally situated with respect to the whole Library rather than ontologically confined to a local region of it. Observer-relative locality belongs to the traversal relation, not to an intrinsic partition of 𝓜. A change of observer, history, resolution, or admissible reference may change the accessible relation without changing the underlying totality.

No intrinsic past/future partition is introduced into 𝓜 at this stage. A sequence of configurations encountered by an observer supplies an ordering of traversal relative to its history and reference. Apparent temporal sequence therefore belongs to the observer-relative relation over the Library rather than to an ontological evolution of the Library itself.

Likewise, an observer may experience increasing complexity, locality, or differentiation as its relational history changes, but this does not imply that 𝓜 itself evolves from simpler to more complex states. The totality remains unchanged while the observer's relation to it changes.

At this stage, $A_O(h,x)$ is a formal construction describing observer-relative accessibility within the Library. It is not identified directly with human sensory experience, phenomenal consciousness, a complete physical universe, or any other specific physical ontology.

**Static Library Invariance.**

$$\forall i,\ \mathcal{M}_i = \mathcal{M}$$

*Observer traversal changes the observer's relation to the Library, not the Library itself.*

**Library Closure.**

$$\mathcal{M} := \mathrm{Closure}_D(\oplus,G)$$

*The Library is the smallest subset of D containing G and closed under the primitive operation ⊕; closure is a mathematical property, not a temporal generation process.*

**Accessible Reality.**

$$A_O(h,x) = \{y \in \mathcal{M} \mid \exists \gamma \in \mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})\ \mathrm{beginning\ at}\ (h,x)\ \mathrm{and\ containing}\ y\ \mathrm{such\ that}\ \mathcal{C}(\gamma \mid O,h,x)=1\}$$

*Accessibility is the existence of at least one observer-coherent traversal through the unchanged Library.*

**Accessibility Subset.**

$$A_O(h,x) \subseteq \mathcal{M}$$

*Every accessible configuration is already a member of the Library.*

**Non-Erasure.**

$$y \notin A_O(h,x) \nRightarrow y \notin \mathcal{M}$$

*Failure of accessibility does not imply absence from the Library.*

**Experienced Reality.**

$$R_O(h,x) := A_O(h,x)$$

*Experienced reality denotes the observer-relative accessible subset without implying that the observer creates it.*

**Formal Reference.**

$$A_O(h,x) = \{y \in \mathcal{M} \mid \exists \gamma \in \mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})\ \mathrm{beginning\ at}\ (h,x)\ \mathrm{and\ containing}\ y\ \mathrm{such\ that}\ \mathcal{C}(\gamma \mid O,h,x)=1\}$$

$$R_O(h,x) := A_O(h,x)$$

$$\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}} := \{x \in \mathcal{M} \mid x\ \mathrm{can\ instantiate\ an\ observer\hbox{-}relative\ reference\ state\ under\ the\ model's\ admissibility\ conditions}\}$$

$$\mathcal{M} := \mathrm{Closure}_D(\oplus,G)$$

$$\forall i,\ \mathcal{M}_i = \mathcal{M}$$

$$A_O(h,x) \subseteq \mathcal{M}$$

$$y \notin A_O(h,x) \nRightarrow y \notin \mathcal{M}$$

**Logical Invariants**

- 𝓜 is the unchanged static totality.
- Observer traversal does not add to or remove from Library membership.
- Accessibility is a relation between observer and Library.
- $A_O(h,x) \subseteq \mathcal{M}$.
- Inaccessibility is not erasure.
- Accessibility is not creation.
- The observer is relationally situated with respect to the whole Library.
- No privileged observer or universal reference point is required.
- Changing observer-relative reference does not alter 𝓜.
- The accessibility construction is domain-neutral.
- The same formal relation may describe physical, AI, financial, or other observer-relative accessible domains.
- The Reference-Point Coherence Proposition is a derived model requirement rather than a theorem of maximality alone.
- No physical time, causality, consciousness, or cosmological interpretation is introduced at this stage.

With membership and accessibility now cleanly separated, the framework has established that an observer's experienced reality is a relation over the unchanged Library rather than a reduction of it. The next subsection takes up the question this separation leaves open: whether an arbitrary admissible reference configuration can always serve as a relational origin without privileging one configuration over another. The answer requires formalizing reference-point neutrality and the coherent continuation that must remain available from any admissible reference.
