## 9. Survivorship and Emergent Lawfulness

The axiomatic floor does not contain a law governing traversal. The Library is defined independently of observer-relative survivorship as the complete XOR-closed totality $\mathcal{M}$. Law-like regularity therefore cannot be inserted as a primitive rule without changing the dependency structure of the framework.

Instead, the framework distinguishes between the existence of candidate continuations within the complete Library and the observer-relative ability to maintain those continuations as coherent traversal. The unfiltered continuation space contains the candidate paths; survivorship restricts that space according to the observer's coherence relation.

**Definition — Unfiltered continuation space.** For $x \in \mathcal{M}$, define $\Omega(x)$ as the subset of the complete path space whose paths begin at $x$.

$$\Omega(x) = \{\gamma \in \mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M}) \mid \gamma\ \mathrm{begins\ at}\ x\}$$

$\Omega(x)$ *is not a generated future or a local ontological neighborhood. It is a reference-indexed subset of an already-complete relational path structure.*

Because $\mathcal{M}$ is static and maximally complete, selecting $x$ as a current relational reference does not cause a new region of the Library to come into existence. The reference simply determines which represented paths count as continuations from that position.

When an observer $O$ has an accumulated history $h$ ending at $x$, continuation is further restricted by observer-relative coherence. The appropriate condition is therefore expressed directly through $\mathcal{C}(\gamma \mid O,h,x) = 1$. The previously used $\gamma_{\mathrm{noise}}$ threshold is eliminated at the foundational level because coherence is already defined through the observer-specific resolution floor $I_N(O)$.

**Observer-relative coherent continuation.**

$$\Gamma_O(h,x) = \{\gamma \in \mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M}) \mid \gamma\ \mathrm{extends}\ h\ \mathrm{from}\ x \land \mathcal{C}(\gamma \mid O,h,x) = 1\}$$

$\Gamma_O(h,x)$ *is a relational filtering of the complete path space. It does not constitute a second ontology and does not replace* $\Omega(x)$.

The distinction is therefore between candidate paths represented in the totality and paths that can remain coherently traversable for a particular observer. A path may belong to $\Omega(x)$ while failing to belong to $\Gamma_O(h,x)$. Such failure does not remove the path from $\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})$, nor does it remove any of its configurations from $\mathcal{M}$.

**Formal Statement — Continuation inclusion.** Observer-relative coherent continuation is a restricted subset of the unfiltered continuation space.

$$\Gamma_O(h,x) \subseteq \Omega(x) \subseteq \mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{M})$$

This construction also preserves the absence of a privileged origin. Any admissible $x \in \mathcal{M}$ may serve as the observer's current relational reference. If coherent continuation existed only from a distinguished configuration, that configuration would acquire the status of a privileged universal origin, contradicting the reference-frame neutrality of the Library.

**Formal Statement — Nonempty coherent continuation.** Under the combined NSFG assumptions, every admissible observer-relative reference configuration admits at least one coherent continuation.

$$\forall x \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{admissible}},\; \Gamma_O(h,x) \neq \varnothing$$

*This is a derived structural requirement of the combined framework. It is not claimed to follow from maximal completeness alone.*

The requirement is stronger than merely asserting that every $x$ belongs to the Library. Library membership guarantees inclusion in the static totality; coherent continuation additionally requires that the observer's present state, history, resolution, and magnitude evaluation admit at least one maintainable path. The distinction between these statements is essential.

The Library therefore remains invariant under traversal. The observer's accessible relation may change as the reference and history change, but the underlying totality does not.

**Library invariance under traversal.**

$$\forall i,\; \mathcal{M}_i = \mathcal{M}$$

*There is no temporal generation of successive Libraries. The index* $i$ *represents an abstract traversal ordering and is not, by itself, physical time.*

Survivorship can now be understood as a filtering relation rather than an ontological deletion mechanism. The paths outside $\Gamma_O(h,x)$ remain part of the complete continuation structure even though they are not maintained as coherent traversal by $O$.

When particular transitions recur across an appropriate family of observer-accessible surviving paths, that recurrence may be described as a regularity. Such regularity is downstream of distinction, resolution, distinguishability, coherence, and survivorship. It therefore need not be inserted as an independent axiom.

**Definition — Observer-relative emergent regularity.** Let $L_O(x,y)$ denote a derived relation in which the transition $x \to y$ is stable and recurring across an appropriate family of $O$-accessible paths.

$$L_O(x,y) = 1 \iff x \to y\ \mathrm{is\ a\ stable\ recurring\ transition\ across\ an\ appropriate\ family\ of}\ O\mathrm{-accessible\ paths}$$

$L_O$ *describes regularity emerging from repeated survivorship. It does not impose a governing law upon the Library.*

The resulting dependency is therefore asymmetric: the axiomatic distinction generates the formal structures from which observer-relative coherence can be defined; coherent survivorship restricts accessible continuation; repeated structure among those survivors can then be described as law-like regularity. The regularity is an observed consequence of the filtering structure, not a premise of it.

**Logical Invariants**

- The Library is complete and unchanged under traversal.
- Candidate continuation exists as a relational structure over the already-complete Library.
- Observer-relative coherence filters accessibility rather than determining Library membership.
- The same observer-relative coherence condition is grounded in $I_N(O)$; no second independent $\gamma_{\mathrm{noise}}$ threshold is required.
- Survivorship does not erase alternatives from the Library.
- No privileged origin is required for coherent traversal.
- Emergent regularity is descriptive and downstream rather than axiomatic.
- The formal dependency remains domain-neutral.

The complete continuation space and the observer-relative coherent continuation relation are now distinguished: the former belongs to the unchanged Library, while the latter is a relational filtering induced by the observer's history, reference, and resolution. With this separation in place, survivorship can be formalized explicitly as a subset relation over continuation space, without deleting the alternatives that remain represented within the Library.
